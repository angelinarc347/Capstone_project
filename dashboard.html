Edge, Fog, and Cloud

System Architecture

How traffic readings move from roadside sensors through nearby processing to city-wide analytics — and keep working if the backhaul link is interrupted.
Traffic SensorsRoadside detectors

Edge Layer

Handles data close to the traffic sensors. It cleans noisy five-minute readings, flags missing or repeated values, and sends updates only when something meaningful changes.

Fog Layer

Processes and analyzes traffic information across nearby road zones. It keeps a local replica of road state, looks five and fifteen minutes ahead, and raises congestion alerts.

Cloud Layer

Provides city-wide analytics, forecasting, and long-term insights. It combines zone summaries, produces the 30-minute forecast, and supports scenario comparison.

How to read these flags

Healthy means the screening rules did not raise a flag. Needs attention is a screening threshold, not a claim that the corresponding real sensor is faulty. Missing readings are generated dropouts in the synthetic raw feed. Unusual readings (candidate spikes) are values changed by at least half the injected spike magnitude relative to the cleaned dataset.

Edge nodes continue local filtering when higher-tier communication is interrupted. Cloud-only control falls back to fixed timing during its simulated WAN outage; edge-only and hierarchical tiers remain locally available.
